# Continent decoration rollout — 15 October 2026

## Scope

The approved Stoneshore sizing is extended to all 92 urban tiles. Decorative
modules use 65% of the preceding trial's dimensions (0.325 of the original kit).
The closest camera span is 192u instead of 240u. Player and NPC gameplay assets
retain their existing sizes. Rural decorative homes use the same smaller scale,
while retaining sparse placement rather than receiving city-density infill.

Urban placement combines region-local district streets, road frontage, inner
rows, courtyards, public spaces and vegetation. It targets 88% of *usable*
frontage after terrain, roads, railways, working plots and building clearance;
this is not 88% of all road length. Stoneshore's three skyline landmarks and
Bauhaus factory remain local features rather than being copied into every city.
The regular map is unchanged.

## Corrections made during rollout

- District and neighbourhood searches are bounded by region/tile so extending
  the trial does not introduce continent-wide pairwise searches.
- Placement protects both authored NPC working plots and the actual rendered
  building mass, including meshes protruding beyond their placement rectangle.
- The Stoneshore depot detour now considers only Stoneshore rail corridors.
  Previously its local-direction calculation accepted distant corridors from
  the expanded network and generated a long road across the sea. Corrected A
  and B routes are approximately 290u and 513u; neither crosses sea.
- The authored 3D road import clips centreline intervals outside the coastline.
  Inland water remains part of the land mask, preserving river/lake bridges.
  Fragment IDs are stable, and subsequent compilation cannot reconnect a sea
  gap. Four additional authored source roads contained sea intervals in the
  initial audit; those intervals are removed rather than redesigned along shore.
- Layout memo saving detects new prepared variants even when shared dictionaries
  have been mutated in place. This prevents a resumed offline bake from
  unnecessarily repeating placement before a usable cache lookup.
- Production packaging rereads the persisted transport snapshot rather than
  saving live roads after sidewalk rendering has annotated them. This preserves
  the same landscape key on the next process launch. A regression test mutates
  live road data and checks that packaging retains the original snapshot.

## Review method

`tools/supply_chain_3d/review_regions.tscn` captures every named region and bounded
unassigned countryside sector at regional/far, medium and near zoom. It exports
the exact graded road segments, visible rail profiles, building footprints and
source keys used for those pictures. The keys can be compared to the installed
production manifest. A separate fresh process checks normal company opening and
the Show all tiles transition with an empty runtime cache.

`audit_regions.py` reports conservative overlap candidates, excluding buried
rails, intended building access, working level crossings and vertically
separated geometry. Its optional mesh pass projects above-ground gameplay asset
triangles instead of treating their whole plot rectangle as solid. It also
measures continuous parallel road/rail overlap exceeding 10u. Neither test
alone proves a visible collision; flagged locations are inspected in renders.
`audit_sea_roads.py` separately checks roads and exposed rails against the
rendered coastline. Contact sheets and a local HTML gallery are generated by
`review_gallery.py`.

## Stoneshore comparison caveat

The refreshed five-tile package has 448 decorative buildings and 86.66% usable
frontage. Actual NPC clearance leaves three generated inner streets instead of
the older eight-street snapshot, with 17 first-row and 46 second-row additions
and no third-row additions. The old strict screenshot harness therefore fails
its count assertions. Its observational mode records all 18 views without
silently weakening those assertions. Geometry checks find no decorative
building/building or building/street intrusions; the seven brownfields remain
clear. Canopy is 10.56%, with 13 beach trees.

The current five-tile package validates independently: 21 resources,
37,114,834 bytes. A fresh process records 19 bundled hits and zero runtime writes:
17.894s process-to-Begin, 1.225s board preparation, and 0.326s first opening.
These measurements ran while the offline continent baker was active, with warm
OS filesystem caches, and are not isolated performance benchmarks.

## Final coverage and density

All 600 terrain tiles are packaged. The review covers all **50 named regions and
31 bounded countryside sectors**, each at far, medium and near zoom (243 regional
captures), plus close-ups for crossing candidates. There are 92 urban tiles,
6,745 actual decorative buildings and 419 gameplay building/warehouse/supplier
records. The placement model also contains public spaces; those are excluded
from the decorative-building total.

Usable road frontage averages **85.01%** (44.18% of all road length). There are
4,856 frontage additions, 811 inner-row buildings, 66 courtyards and 296 green
spaces. Two urban tiles have no authored road frontage: `tile_20_6` in Kingstown
and `tile_6_19` on the southwest island. Two tiles fall just below 75% usable
frontage: `tile_15_2` (72.56%) and `tile_23_2` (70.68%). No roads were invented
solely to force these coverage statistics.

The coast audit finds **zero road segments and zero exposed rail segments over
the sea**. The six rail crossing candidates below are inland; they are distinct
from the removed giant sea detour. Coast clipping removes sea intervals but does
not redesign their remaining road ends along shore.

## Installed bake and fresh-process verification

The installed full package contains **1,840 resources, 1,899,582,184 bytes
(1.77 GiB)** across 600 tiles, including 34 auxiliary resources for the default
owned-company opening. Medium terrain textures decode to 2,241,286,200 bytes if
all 600 are resident; near textures decode to 8,965,279,800 bytes. These are total
texture payloads, not measured game memory; normal detail streaming retains a
subset. Near resources occupy 1,060,484,655 bytes on disk.

Independent validation passes with current source fingerprints and no errors.
The initial validation caught one underestimated terrain-cut bound on
`tile_10_5`; the packager now encloses actual terrain meshes from all three LODs.
A metadata-only repair widened that tile's visibility bounds, retained all art,
and was independently revalidated. A regression test covers the low cut and
unchanged valid bounds. The 133.33 MiB combined continent resource is tracked by
Git LFS; the game loads the native resource, not an LFS pointer.

A fresh Godot 4.6.2 / Forward+ process used the installed package and an empty
runtime cache path. No offline task baker/reviewer was active; OS filesystem
caches were warm. A separate user-owned Godot process was left running. Results:

| Measurement | Time / result |
|---|---:|
| Process start to Begin | 19.045s |
| Owned board preparation during loading | 3.031s |
| First supply-view opening after Begin | 0.188s |
| Show all tiles, detail ready | **16.023s** |
| Full board preparation within that transition | 14.274s |
| Bundled hits after owned opening | 35 |
| Bundled hits after full switch and additional close-up | 183 |
| Cache misses / runtime bake writes | **0 / 0** |
| Runtime cache directory created | No |

The Show all tiles transition is **not below five seconds**. Full-board profile
stages include model/memo restoration (4.551s), key/transport work (4.264s),
scenery loading (0.984s), combined continent loading (0.758s), standing data
(1.854s) and scene construction (2.681s). Some named sub-stages overlap; do not
sum nested timings. Remaining work is primarily restoration, hashing and scene
assembly, not missing texture bakes. This rollout does not claim to solve it.

Offline generation is separate from normal loading. The final resumed assembly
took 1,106.064s after preparation, reusing completed and generated-cache resources
rather than rebuilding everything. Earlier cold preparation took approximately
1,179s. The 243 visual captures were made during offline generation and are not
used as normal-loading benchmarks. Their content keys match the final package.

154 focused GDScript assertions pass, including coast clipping, local depot
routing/layout memo reuse, placement, camera, crossings and packaging. Python
audit fixtures and syntax checks pass. This is not a claim that the entire
repository test suite or the old strict Stoneshore count snapshot passes (see
comparison caveat above). Nonfatal certificate/exit warnings remain.
