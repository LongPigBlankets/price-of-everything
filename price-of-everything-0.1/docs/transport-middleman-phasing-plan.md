## Assessment

The proposal is worth prototyping. It gives new players a complete business loop before asking them to manage transport, while extending the game's make-or-buy choice to logistics. Separating ownership, destination and operator is the right foundation for mixed companies. The explicit treatment of construction, cash reconciliation and in-flight goods also protects recent fixes.

The clarified objective is one inclusive **Middleman fee**, absorbing transport, distance, congestion, port handling, warehousing within each building’s outsourced buy/sell service. The player starts by managing their factory and its trading margin. When they choose to operate logistics functions themselves, the corresponding costs and constraints become theirs to manage. This is the central acceptance criterion for every phase, not just a reporting change. The middleman is strictly a building-level buyer/seller: no transport of player-owned goods, including within one tile. Any reference below to outsourced fallback means separate market procurement/sale, never a carrier service.

Balance constraint, 19 September: preserve the established input ratios and energy requirements. Review broad goods-price scaling, tactical output gains around 10%, and selective prices for dependency-audited terminal goods before changing chain quantities. The [August / recent balance review](reviews/middleman-balance-design-2026-09-19.md) distinguishes historical design artifacts from deployed tuning and records analytical alternatives. Congestion traversal accounting is now corrected; the Pepper Valley runner uses v2 snapshots while preserving v1 pre-fix evidence. Its three-owned-rail case is the preferred ownership comparison. No balance candidate is deployed by this review. The subsequent [targeted trial](reviews/targeted-output-gains-2026-09-19.md) applies six modest output gains and two terminal-car price increases via a reversible manifest; Pepper Valley now has a separately versioned 33-output contract and v3 results. Inputs, energy, staffing and construction kits remain unchanged.

| Arrangement | What the player pays and manages |
| --- | --- |
| Fully outsourced operating loop | Input goods, factory operating costs and one Middleman fee; receives goods-sale revenue. Provider storage, traffic, port handling and transport are covered. |
| Own warehouse, generic carrier | Player stock/capacity and warehouse costs plus generic-carrier freight. Middleman fees apply only to separate building-level market trades. |
| Own road/rail operations with generic-carrier legs | Fleet/equipment, fuel, distance, access, congestion, storage and carrier charges on remaining legs. Middleman is not a carrier for these goods. |
| Direct port trade | Applicable port fees, routes, transport, congestion, storage and settlement delay are exposed for that operation. Any other outsourced business retains its inclusive fee. |

The fee can be quoted per transaction and attributed per building while appearing as one aggregate category in company accounts. It need not be a single flat subscription or a single end-of-turn debit. Do not surface separate provider cost components as player obligations. Owning one warehouse must not expose port fees on an unrelated outsourced sale. An ownership-switch preview compares the inclusive fee avoided against **all** newly assumed costs and working capital.

The principal risk is scope: this combines a change to production funding and settlement with storage ownership, fleet simulation, direct-trade economics, policy controls, graph semantics and tutorial scenarios. Treat it as several playable increments with economic gates. The first milestone should prove that an inland factory can fund one batch, run once and receive an understandable same-turn result. Warehouse and fleet work should follow evidence that this loop works.

Same-turn settlement improves working capital and startup timing, not necessarily recurring profit. The earlier [cash-flow comparison](reviews/middleman-cashflow-2026-09-12.md) is useful evidence of the problem, but its tariff is illustrative and its calculations exclude several operating costs. It cannot establish balanced intermediary rates or an attractive return on owned logistics.

## Recommended decisions for the prototype

These are proposed working defaults, not decisions already established by implementation.

1. **Funding:** current cash plus explicitly drawn, available credit; no borrowing against anticipated same-turn sales. Give production a deterministic budget after existing due obligations and construction claims. Define treatment of predictable running costs so the startup quote does not imply that inputs are the entire cash requirement.
2. **Allocation:** use a documented stable building order and accept complete feasible batches. Admit a batch only if all its ingredients, fees and other required resources fit. Where the existing simulation derates output, plan its actual permitted quantities. Recompute fees per accepted building transaction before committing; do not charge rejected batches or net trades between buildings.
3. **Timing:** newly produced physical goods remain unavailable to other factories until the next turn initially; retain existing power behaviour. Direct-mode buildings count landed, unreserved stock, not expected local output. Fully outsourced buildings use only their own private service holdings before procurement, never shared tile output. Sell after the production pass so sales cannot finance another purchase/production loop.
4. **Unused purchases:** reserve inputs for a specific operation. If execution subsequently fails, keep the paid goods as a visible player-owned holding with no duplicate rebuy. Make its reuse, release and storage treatment explicit; it must not become unlimited free general storage or silently disappear.
5. **Tariffs:** build the 0.5% dynamic fee plus class tariff × location factor per traded unit, on both purchases and sales. Selected class tariffs are £0.05 solid-heavy and £0.50 ultra-heavy; storage is included with no fixed per-cycle/storage surcharge. Keep £40/£76 and 0.4% cases as historical controls. Use snapshotted `MarketState.get_price()` before buy markup, ordinary buy/sale prices and float ledger precision (UI rounding only); the initial supported fixture uses Pepper Valley coefficient 1.5 and steel/wire/motors.
6. **Service eligibility:** define coverage and supported goods, including pipeline-class goods, independently of player port ownership. Test inland service without a player-built road. Retain applicable import restrictions; decide deliberately whether any restriction represents global imports only rather than domestic intermediary supply.
7. **Compatibility:** old saves remain on legacy logistics initially; new intermediary games use a persisted ruleset key. Prototype saves are labelled experimental and are not toggled back to legacy in place. No automatic conversion of outstanding trades, stock or warehouse upgrades.
8. **Capacity:** fully outsourced market operations require no player fleet, port or warehouse capacity management. For owned operations, introduce hard fleet availability in the road phase while retaining existing network congestion pricing initially. Replacing network pricing with hard throughput queues requires a separate decision. Later owned-fleet fallback is a separately priced generic-carrier service under a visible cap; own-only waits for fleet availability. It is never an implicit middleman trade.

Before warehouse work, settle operating-buffer limits, owned local handling latency/capacity and the warehouse/Hub responsibility boundary. Before fleet work, settle workload units, return/unloading occupancy, equipment goods and fallback spending caps. Before direct-trade work, set entry-cost and payback targets. Pumping Stations can remain deferred.

## Turn-order assessment

**Recommendation: keep the top-level phase sequence and refactor the order inside `PROCESS`.** The current internal sequence cannot provide the proposed operating loop unchanged. A new global phase is unnecessary for the prototype and would expand changes to UI, animation, tests and settled-result consumers without solving the actual dependency by itself.

The outer sequence in `turn_manager.gd` is:

`DECIDE → PROCESS → SEND → AI → NARRATIVE → RECEIVE → turn_advanced → DECIDE → turn_resolution_completed`

The labels are not the economic execution order. `Production._process_transport_arrivals()` runs near the start of `PROCESS`, including congestion snapshotting, shipment advancement and arrival bills/receipts. Construction then advances/claims materials. Production follows; output-bound sales can execute during it. Grid settlement, buffered output flush, recurring moves, input replenishment, stockpile sales, expenses, loan payments, taxes and reports also happen inside `PROCESS`.

`AI` records company rankings from the settled production summary. `NARRATIVE` evaluates research, events, modifiers and decisions. Market prices advance on `turn_advanced`; the completed turn's trade volumes must be recorded before that. The inspected `SEND`/`RECEIVE` phases do not own shipment dispatch/arrival settlement. Moving `RECEIVE` before `PROCESS` would therefore not supply inputs earlier.

Recommended internal sequence under the new ruleset:

| Step | Work and required ordering |
| --- | --- |
| **1. Prepare and settle existing commitments** | Retain labour policy, survey and battery preparation. Initialize the turn ledger/power state. Dispatch reserved construction orders and advance physical shipments exactly once at their existing start-of-turn point; settle their bills/receipts and retry unloading. Advance construction/upgrades and claim their materials before allocating operating inputs. Preserve construction's existing delivery/countdown semantics. |
| **2. Plan the operating cycle** | Snapshot effective policies and current prices after arrivals and construction. Determine eligible buildings, scaled input requirements and power/deposit constraints. Separate private middleman holdings from shared direct stock/feed buffers; reserve deterministically and plan one purchased shortfall per building. Account for funded power producers needed by consumers without executing a second production cycle. |
| **3. Reserve funding and supply inputs** | Protect due obligations and reserve complete affordable batches plus their purchase-side inclusive fee. Explicitly draw eligible credit if needed, record the liability and cash inflow, then purchase inputs into operation-specific reservations. No anticipated sales finance this step. Rejected batches leave neither a charge nor a market-volume record. |
| **4. Produce with the existing bounded cascade** | Preserve `has_run`, the maximum-pass guard and power-producer/consumer dependencies: multiple dependency passes can occur, but each building operates at most once. Record output intents instead of immediately selling intermediary-bound goods. Physical outputs, including surplus/failure remainders, cannot leak into another factory's current input reservation. Preserve unused purchased inputs if execution fails. |
| **5. Route outputs and settle trade** | Keep grid/intermittency dependencies. Flush retained outputs and dispatch eligible physical transfers after this turn's arrival advancement; new trips do not gain a free transit tick. Run legacy/direct replenishment only where still selected, excluding intermediary inputs. Collect supported building-level intermediary sales separately from direct stockpile sales. Group intermediary records by building/good/direction/turn/order ID without netting across buildings, settle actual output and its fee, and record trade volume once. Do not re-enter input planning after receipts arrive. |
| **6. Close accounting** | Charge factory costs and only applicable player-operated logistics costs. Add all actual Middleman fee entries to one reporting total; purchase-side fees already paid in step 3 are not paid again. Preserve existing loan/tab, tax, carbon and cost-solver dependencies. Publish a reconciled summary before `AI` and `NARRATIVE`. |

Important integration work within this sequence:

- **Funding is a simulation operation.** `purchase_headroom()` includes borrowing capacity, but `SolvencyState` normally bridges negative cash only on `turn_resolution_completed`, after sales and reports, and its bridge is disabled headless. The prototype must use the same explicit funding path in headless and windowed play. Respect loan minimums and available capacity, and define when a loan drawn during step 3 first ages/pays: moving issuance earlier must not accidentally consume a grace turn or charge an unexpected first instalment.
- **Financing is not sales revenue.** Add new funding draws to the cash reconciliation without inflating operating profit, taxes or the rankings' `money_in` input. Audit `cash_change_of()`, cost summaries and all readers. Existing building-credit tabs synthetically refund inputs later in `PROCESS`; integrate any supported tab funding with reservations so it cannot fund the same batch twice. Keep post-resolution bridge/rescue movements separately attributable, and verify autosave/solvency callback ordering against final cash.
- **The middleman must bypass covered billing paths.** `queue_buy()` and `MarketState.execute_sale()` commit legacy port/freight charges. Reuse their relevant pricing, policy and volume logic through explicit service-aware paths, not by setting travel time to zero. The warehousing loop must charge owned retained stock only, excluding provider inventory and covered service holdings.
- **Power feasibility needs a shared planner.** Do not reject a consumer merely because a funded power producer has not executed yet, or purchase for consumers whose power cannot be supplied. Preserve existing battery preparation, grid rules and next-turn intermittency calculations. Prototype fixtures need both pre-supplied power and a fuel-consuming generator.
- **One fee category can contain several settlements.** Input fees are paid before production; output fees can be deducted when proceeds settle. Both contribute to one company total. Idempotency and final attribution must prevent duplicated quantities/fees, including failed batches and output remainders; the current tariff has no fixed transaction component.
- **Prices advance once after settlement.** The market currently accumulates buy/sell volumes and applies their effect when the turn advances. Retain that boundary; do not tick prices between input purchase and output sale or count internal transfers as market trades.

Keep internal stages as explicit, directly ordered simulation calls with profiler markers and a turn context. Avoid adding new unordered signal listeners for funding or purchase supply. Preserve existing phase enums and public signals for the first implementation. Consider new top-level phases only if a later feature needs interleaved external actors, independent resumable stages or a deliberate turn-presentation redesign; that would require migrating dependent listeners together.

## Validation and economic gates

Preserve baseline runs before changing simulation. Compare identical recipes, locations, starting funds and seeds under both rulesets: metals, glass/windows, motors, hydraulics, ingredient-heavy recipes and low-value bulk goods. Include port-side, connected inland and locations without player-built transport. Test low cash, blocked power, shared inputs, mixed providers, unavailable fuel, full warehouses and policy changes during transit.

Record first-sale turn, minimum starting cash, idle turns, input/fee outlay, operating profit, borrowing/interest, peak inventory, cash tied up in transit and the payback of each ownership investment. Use real resolved turns rather than quote-only arithmetic. Agree acceptable startup survival and payback ranges before evaluating the experiments; no numerical target is established by the proposal yet.

Required invariants:

- Fully outsourced operation has one Middleman fee and no separate provider freight, congestion, port, handling or warehousing bill. Unused legacy transport/warehouse state cannot block its operating cycle. Taking a function in-house reveals only the costs and constraints of that function; mixed operations never double-charge covered work.
- Phase-order scenarios cover a zero-stock inland startup, funded power producer plus consumer, construction completing this turn, old shipments arriving, unaffordable multi-input batches, no production re-entry after sales, new shipments advancing next turn, and fee settlement preceding taxes/reports. Headless and windowed funding must agree. Verify both outer signal order and inner execution order.
- Closing cash equals opening cash plus receipts and financing inflows minus purchases, service charges, operating costs and financing outflows, with construction movements accounted for separately and exactly once.
- Opening goods plus acquisitions and production equal consumption, disposals and closing owned goods across stock, reserved holdings and transit. Any intended loss must be explicit.
- Every quantity has one owner and one authoritative order/transfer assignment. A provider change does not change destination or create a sale.
- Splitting API/UI orders cannot change the consolidated bill; shared inventory, credit and fleet capacity cannot be reserved twice. Negative-net sales and zero-quantity orders have defined handling.
- Quote and execution share pricing/allocation rules. State changes invalidate or recompute a quote; UI estimates never mutate simulation.
- Construction prepayment, same-turn cancellation, retained land and loan associations remain intact. Ordinary running purchases do not become exceptional-cost warnings.
- Save/load between turns preserves order IDs, holdings, overrides and fleet occupancy. Loading must not repeat settlement. Continue the existing DECIDE-only save boundary; mid-resolution saving is outside scope.

Use the existing unit runner (`python3 tools/run_tests.py` from the Godot project root), with focused production/market/stockpile/transport/finance/save-load/commitments/construction suites during their phases, then the full suite at release gates. Run `tools/parse_check.tscn` and the existing 100-turn `tests/e2e_stoneshore.tscn` separately; add a new intermediary scenario because the legacy scenario cannot establish new-rule correctness. Inspect changed UI and both tutorial paths in a windowed build. Measure turn time and graph readability at larger company sizes.

## Historical £40 tariff progression evidence (superseded tariff)

The historical [middleman-port contract](../tests/scenarios/pepper_valley_motors_middleman_ports.json) and v5 snapshots recorded the earlier move from £20 to £40 and removal of introductory port relief. The fixed £40 tariff is now superseded by the dynamic tariff; the new-ruleset port-rate decision remains current. Fees are £16 inputs + £16 outputs + £8 warehousing, one total with a breakdown. `ruleset.logistics_model = "middleman_v1"` uses the normal 3% base port rate from turn one; the provider replaces early port relief. The rule is saved with the match, and legacy/tutorial saves retain their existing economy pending explicit migration. The flag does not itself supply middleman gameplay.

The [progression review](reviews/middleman-fee40-progression-2026-09-19.md) compares turns 10–19, the turn-40 baseline, output-only improvement, and output plus actual Depot Scheduling / Groupage rewards. The provider reference remains arithmetic. Old v1–v4 snapshots remain history, including the abandoned £40-plus-introductory-port combination.

Acceptance must distinguish run-rate profit from investment recovery. A fully prebuilt three-owned-section rail corridor is nearly tied with the provider before research; at turns 10–19 it is still £0.57 ahead because port growth has not accumulated, while at turns 40–49 it is £0.009 behind. The tested output-plus-research rail case crosses by £1.43/turn, but roads do not yet cross. Do not call these results a complete balance sign-off: require a meaningful early outsourcing advantage and later positive savings with credible capex/working-capital payback. Cover government construction timing, upgraded links, owned fleet costs and actual research acquisition, keeping recipe inputs and the recent yield/price trial intact.
