# Balance v4 manual playtest

The balance-v4 values are deployed directly in the runtime goods, recipe and research CSVs. `scripts/catalog.gd` loads the goods and recipes at game startup; research descriptions are also loaded from CSV. Restart the game and begin a new Open Field run after switching presets so a test does not mix catalogs or inherited company state.

## Switch the game data

Run these commands from `price-of-everything-0.1`:

```bash
python3 tools/manage_balance_v4_data.py --status
python3 tools/manage_balance_v4_data.py --revert
python3 tools/manage_balance_v4_data.py --apply
```

- `--status` reports `deployed`, `reverted`, `mixed`, or `conflict` without editing anything.
- `--revert` restores the exact 58 pre-v4 field values recorded from baseline commit `aabb4439e604e286d9854870bf711732a71d3b43`.
- `--apply` redeploys the accepted v4 values.
- A switch refuses to overwrite any field that is neither its recorded old nor new value. This protects later manual CSV edits instead of silently destroying them.

The field-level archive is `data/balance_v4_changes.json`. Generated `.translation` and `.import` sidecars are not the source of truth and may be regenerated by Godot after a CSV switch.

## Motor strategy A: prudent £1,200 road benchmark

Use this benchmark for the current live-rule comparison. It retains the standing extraction penalties, ignores earned economic modifiers, permits normal building pauses and paid upgrades, and never sells or demolishes a building.

The expansion rule is:

- Calculate the trailing average post-tax profit over the last three completed turns.
- When cash is short, take an expansion loan only when total active expansion-loan interest plus the proposed loan's interest per turn is strictly less than 50% of that average. The comparison uses interest per turn (`principal × rate ÷ 36`), not the repayment including principal.
- If the trailing average is zero or negative, do not borrow for expansion. Buy a building or upgrade only when cash is strictly greater than 1.5 times its full project cost: land, building price and expected market-purchased construction materials.
- Survival bridge loans are reported separately and never count as strategy-approved expansion loans.

Reference sequence:

1. Start blank with £1,200 and construct the road from `tile_23_10` through the `tile_24_9` hub to Capital Port at `tile_24_7`.
2. Build Water Pumping and one coal power plant on `tile_23_10`. Buy coal initially. Pause the water pump at 100 water and resume it at 40 so surplus water does not fill the shared warehouse.
3. Build Pig Iron Smelting and sell iron ingots until cash reaches £600.
4. Add Steelmaking and retain £700 before the next stage; route pig iron internally and sell surplus steel.
5. Add Copper Blistering and retain £800, then Copper Wire Drawing and retain £800.
6. Add Motor Manufacture, sell motors, and begin raw integration immediately rather than waiting for a larger cash balance. Prioritise local coal for power, then iron, then hub coal; the test uses a separate unbounded coal field at `tile_20_9` rather than exhausting three mines on one 2,000-unit deposit.

Current automated result:

| Checkpoint | Result |
|---|---:|
| First motor production | Turn 74 |
| Motors sold | 1,204 |
| Approved expansion loans | 1 (£9.21 principal; £0.026/turn interest) |
| Loan-interest rejections | 0 |
| Loss-making 1.5× cash waits | 21 |
| Emergency bridge loans | 32 |
| Bankruptcy | Turn 113 |
| Full raw integration | Not reached |

This is an expected-red balance result. The rule greatly reduces strategic borrowing and extends survival beyond the legacy road benchmark, but £1,200 still does not fund full raw integration under the live mine penalties without eventually relying on emergency debt.

## Motor strategy B: legacy £1,200 road benchmark

This is the fastest tested motor opening, retained as a legacy comparison. It neutralizes all modifiers, including the standing extraction penalties, so do not compare its mine yields directly with a live game.

1. Start with £1,200 and buy an industrial hub two land tiles from a port. The automated reference uses `tile_24_9` with `tile_24_7` as its port.
2. Construct four buildings on the hub:
   - Furnace — `r_003` Steelmaking
   - Furnace — `r_007` Copper Smelting
   - Industrial Factory — `r_008` Copper Wire Drawing
   - Industrial Factory — `r_009` Motor Manufacture
3. Route steel to Motor Manufacture, copper ingots to Copper Wire Drawing, and copper wiring to Motor Manufacture.
4. Sell motors and surplus steel to market. Let the opening recipes purchase missing raw inputs from the market.
5. Run the four-building company for approximately eight turns before adding infrastructure. Record cash, live profit and remaining loan capacity each turn.
6. Build roads from the hub to the port and the adjacent coal-and-water tile. Integrate in this order: coal extraction for processing, water, first coal power, a second coal mine, second coal power, iron extraction, pig iron, then copper extraction. Add the iron and copper roads only when their mines are about to be constructed.

Automated reference result, without upgrades or modifiers:

| Checkpoint | Result |
|---|---:|
| First motor production | Turn 6 |
| Motors sold before failure | 1,092 |
| Turn-30 cash | £53.52 |
| Turn-30 live profit | −£8.53 |
| Bankruptcy | Turn 40 |
| Furthest completed integration | First coal mine |

The reference bankruptcy condition is exhausted borrowing capacity plus negative cash and negative profit for five consecutive turns. It never sells or demolishes buildings. Building upgrades are allowed for a manual run, but the automated reference does not use them; record any upgrade and its turn when comparing results.

This scenario-specific £1,200 balance does not alter the normal Open Field starting-cash preset.

## Motor strategy C: £1,500 rail benchmark

The earlier reference locates its hub three tiles from port and uses rail for remote ore. It produces motors on turn 8, sells 420, and reaches bankruptcy on turn 28 after adding water. The legacy road benchmark therefore gives the motor company more runway despite its lower starting cash, but neither version reaches full integration.

## Motor strategy D: minimum downstream opening

For the comparison case, construct only Steelmaking and Motor Manufacture, sell their peak output, and purchase copper wiring from the market until Copper Smelting and Copper Wire Drawing can be added.

The automated reference produced motors on turn 8 but sold only 308 before bankruptcy on turn 15. Purchased wiring consumes working capital too quickly, so this is a useful negative control rather than the recommended route.

## What to record

For a useful comparison, record the following at the end of every construction stage:

- turn, cash, live profit and remaining loan capacity;
- buildings owned and their upgrade levels;
- first turn each target recipe runs;
- motors sold and any surplus steel or copper output sold;
- grid power bought and owned power generated;
- rail, cable, pipe and reinforced-pipe tiles constructed;
- any five-turn bankruptcy streak.

The automated portfolio report is regenerated with:

```bash
python3 tools/run_balance_v4_e2e.py
```

Its consolidated result is written to `reports/balance/balance_v4_e2e_results.md`.
