# Loading hex carousel

Six L3 buildings on individual capsule-art hexes: factory, furnace, power plant,
petrochemical refinery, farm and offshore wind farm. Land uses the capsule's green turf;
offshore uses its sea column. The soil, coal and ore boundaries are generated by
`tools/steam_capsule/earth_plate.py`, and the stack puffs by its `smoke.py`.

The renders use the current tracked building builders, including the replacement furnace,
power plant and refinery, rather than the old capsule's building models. Each building is
centred and scaled within 88% of the hex's inscribed circle. Its lowest geometry is seated
0.012 units above the top, preventing a gap or a second floating ground slab. Offshore's
old rectangular sea surface is replaced by the hex.

The camera and geometry stay identical between looks. Warm light arrives from the upper
left in daytime; a cool night fill leaves the buildings readable, with existing window
panes lit, industrial heat/flare cues and small nacelle beacons. The factory's sloped
skylights and annex roof panes light up within their existing mullions. The power plant
retains transparent roof glazing over warm work lights illuminating its turbine sets.
The furnace's recessed working bays and molten ladles have yellow centres, orange/red
edges and local light spill in both looks. The refinery flare casts warm light onto its
shield, tower and nearby equipment in both looks. Each offshore turbine has a red nacelle
light and a yellow-white light by its boat landing, with local spill at night. These are
offline lights; runtime rendering uses the day/night images and a small smoke atlas.
At night, the columns' painted shade bands and print stippling are lifted according to
their orientation and distance from the flare; faces turned away retain their shading.
The face-orientation print pass uses the same stipple method as the capsule and sprites.
Brick materials also receive the game's catalogue coursing: 0.075m courses, 0.21m
stretcher bond and 0.005m pale joints, in source model coordinates. That procedural paint
is normally supplied by the 3D shader; flat Blender material colours alone omit it.

From the repository root, with background `blender`, Python, Pillow and NumPy available:

```sh
python3 tools/loading_carousel/build.py --out /absolute/review-directory --install
```

Raw colour/shade renders and per-building logs remain in the review directory. The twelve
finished 1024px RGBA smoke-free stills, six smoke atlases, `smoke-layouts.json` and
`provenance.json` install in
`price-of-everything-0.1/assets/loading/carousel/`. The manifest records input source/model
and output hashes. Rebuild after updating any featured building. Existing texture import
sidecars retain desktop high-quality VRAM compression and mipmaps for the stills.
Smoke atlases use BC7 without mipmaps to keep adjacent animation cells from bleeding.

`scripts/loading_carousel.gd` shows each building for five seconds: daylight to 0.5s,
dissolve over three seconds to night by 3.5s, hold until 4.3s, then slide left to reveal
the next hex to the right. Six buildings make a 30-second loop. Current and next
day/night pairs and their smoke atlases are the only resident textures. The next group
loads on a worker; a late read holds the current tile. Static building holds do not queue
redraws; smoking tiles update one animation uniform. A live 3D viewport and video decoder
are unnecessary.

`loading_screen.gd` keeps script warming, threaded scene loading and readiness gating.
Begin appears as soon as the world and pending save are ready, independently of the art.
The DS2 cream key remains beneath the artwork. The retired film and intro images remain
available as source material but are excluded by all three release export presets.
The tiles are enlarged by up to 50%, constrained by vertical clearance for smoke and
the button; no building-name or level caption is drawn.

From the game directory:

```sh
python3 tools/run_tests.py --test _test_loading_
AGENT_GODOT_WINDOW=1 godot --path . res://tools/loading_carousel_probe.tscn -- --no-telemetry
CAROUSEL_PLAY=1 AGENT_GODOT_WINDOW=1 godot --path . res://tools/loading_carousel_probe.tscn -- --no-telemetry
CAROUSEL_RECORD=1 CAROUSEL_OUTPUT=/absolute/frames AGENT_GODOT_WINDOW=1 godot --path . res://tools/loading_carousel_probe.tscn -- --no-telemetry
```

The default probe captures all twelve looks, the mid-slide, Begin and a portrait window.
Play mode runs a fresh New Game through Begin into gameplay. Record mode writes 900 JPEG
frames for a 30fps review movie; these are review outputs, not game assets. Normal loading
can still stall during indivisible main-thread world work; the carousel does not change
that underlying workload.

The recorder verifies the window and image size before saving every frame. A review movie
from 18 October contained one 3600x2260 frame (frame 54) among its 1280x720 images while
macOS finished restoring the saved display size. This caused the factory's brief apparent
width change. The recorder now retries such captures without advancing the review clock;
it does not resize a bad frame into the movie or change the runtime carousel's geometry.

### Rising smoke

Factory and furnace smoke, and both the power plant's flue and cooling-tower steam, rise
in a separate layer. The original capsule plume frames the fixed camera, then the base
renders without smoke. Twelve periodic puff frames are rendered for each look, with the
building and terrain as holdouts so rims/equipment correctly occlude smoke. Existing
material slots and face indices are preserved when temporarily applying holdouts.

Each building's puffs share one tightly cropped, square 256px cell; the twelve cells pack
into a 1024x768 atlas per look. The shader interpolates adjacent frames, blends day/night
with the same factor as the building, and composites using premultiplied alpha. No live
particles, new puff nodes, per-frame texture uploads or extra draw surfaces are needed.
The puff-age construction is periodic with slots permuted across the 1.5s loop boundary.
Puffs emerge from a fixed mouth cluster, grow, drift upward and shrink away at the end.

Two BC7 atlases cost 1.5 MiB per smoking building, with no atlas mipmaps. Current/next
groups add at most 3 MiB to the approximately 5.33 MiB still-texture budget. A tile without
smoke neither loads nor samples atlases. Previous atlases are released on advance. Busy
workers alone need not stop the loop, but main-thread loading stalls still pause frame
submission, just like the fade/slide. No world-loading scheduling change is claimed.

On 19 October, a fresh Metal process at 1280x720 measured the animated carousel for
10 seconds after settling: 1450 frames, 6.994ms p95, 8.616ms worst and zero frames over
100ms. This is presentation-only frame timing, not a new game-loading benchmark. All
six day/night smoke sequences fit within the camera and their closing frame difference
is no larger than an ordinary adjacent-frame difference. The geometry itself is checked
at phase 0 and 1 during baking and must match under permutation of the puff slots.

## Loading profile and proposed split (18 October 2026)

### Corrected demo comparison

The original profiler hardcoded the legacy `coal_baron` start without the demo menu's
rules. That was not representative of the shipped Metal Magnate demo, whose initial
world has a matching packaged snapshot. The 47.89s figure below must not be compared
with a normal demo's roughly 20s startup or attributed to the new carousel.

The probe now obtains the selected start and overrides from the actual New Game panel;
`CAROUSEL_START` is an explicit optional override for other scenarios. A controlled
comparison changed only `loading_screen.gd` between the pre-carousel version at
`478043592` and the new version. Both fresh processes used the current game/building
assets, High quality, Metal Magnate, Normal, the 100-turn demo timeline, and separate
empty runtime-cache directories. Both reported the same viewport sequence (1280x720,
then 3600x2260); the requested initial size was not maintained by the capture window.

| Presentation | Time to Begin | 3D preparation | Worst frame |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Old video and intro plates | 17.950s | 4.831s | 4.536s |
| New hex carousel | 17.241s | 4.556s | 4.562s |

This pair shows no startup slowdown from the carousel; the small difference is not
enough evidence to claim a repeatable speed improvement. Neither run recalculated
the 13 company tiles seen in the legacy stress case. The old/new comparison is a
time-to-Begin measurement: both probes subsequently hit existing background-resource
teardown errors and exited 134, so it is not a clean-shutdown validation.

Prioritise the common 4.5s stall, present under both screens, before treating the
legacy scenario's 9s layout solve as the ordinary demo workload. Preserve the valid
Metal Magnate package; investigate other exposed starts separately before deciding
whether additional initial overlays need baking.

### Legacy-start stress case (not the shipped demo benchmark)

Measured on the local Apple M5 Pro, Godot 4.6.2 / Metal, fresh processes, the same
`coal_baron` start, with telemetry disabled. The capture window resizes early from
1280x720 to the saved 3600x2260; the standalone steady run starts after window settling.
Frame intervals include all main-loop activity, not just the carousel. These are local
measurements, not promised load times on players' machines.

| Run | Time to Begin / observation | Frames over 100ms | Worst frame |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| High quality | 47.89s | 42 | 9.140s |
| Low quality | 13.73s | 18 | 4.682s |
| Low repeat, with draw timing | 13.38s | 17 | 4.593s |
| Carousel alone, after settling | 10.01s observed | 0 | 10.9ms |

An earlier High run took 69.65s. The trace explains the variable cost:

- Script warming: 38 scripts, 2.56s across multiple frames. Threaded scene resource
  loading itself took 278ms; instantiating/attaching the scene remains main-thread work.
- The starting-building phase spans 6.3s. Its worst common stall is 4.59–4.68s. The
  detailed Low repeat measured only 1.027ms between render pre/post signals on that
  worst frame. This points to main-loop CPU work, rather than the still-image draw.
  More per-callback instrumentation is needed to attribute that whole stall precisely;
  the coarse phase timing does not prove one placement function consumed all of it.
- High's supply-chain preparation phase takes 34.81s. Within it, 13 changed company
  tiles spend 27.56s in synchronous layout solves. The slowest tiles are `tile_9_9`
  (9.138s), `tile_6_14` (5.055s), `tile_9_8` (4.582s), `tile_6_13` (3.889s) and
  `tile_6_12` (3.085s). Their frame gaps closely match their solve durations.
- `tile_overlay.gd` yields between changed tiles, but `_solve_layout()` runs an entire
  tile synchronously. `road_placement.gd` then calls `FrontageFill.run()` synchronously;
  the trace's long gaps concentrate in frontage/infill fitting. A per-tile yield is
  therefore too coarse. Merely adding more yields around that call will not bound it.

Recommended loading work, in order (analysis/proposal; not implemented here):

1. Bake the fixed initial overlays for each shipped demo start, checking the current
   fingerprint before preparing layout. Retain the existing generic fallback for saves
   and modified starts. Validate/install affected production packages with the normal
   bake pipeline and verify a fresh process makes no unexpected runtime bake writes.
2. Split dynamic layout into immutable input capture, worker-owned computation, and
   main-thread application. Start with frontage/infill candidate searches. Workers own
   their model, spatial indexes, query data and result; they do not access the live
   SceneTree or mutate the shared `_solved_tiles` memo. Commit only if the generation
   token still matches. Drain cancelled workers before freeing their inputs.
3. Make fallback candidate searches resumable below tile granularity. Apply nodes,
   meshes and resource uploads in approximately 2–4ms batches, yielding when the elapsed
   budget is spent. Bound the work inside a single candidate/mesh too; yielding once
   after an expensive tile or panel is insufficient. Preserve deterministic placement
   order/seeds and compare geometry fingerprints.
4. Instrument the remaining common 4.6s stall by callback before refactoring the
   starting-building path. Retain existing bulk signal/redraw coalescing; a return to
   one-building-per-frame previously made the whole load slower.
5. Drive presentation independently of load completion: day hold → short dusk blend →
   night hold → slide. Prefetch only the next pair. Schedule remaining unavoidable
   commits during a still hold and gate the start of fades/slides on quiet frames. Such
   holds can exceed five seconds under pressure; Begin must still appear immediately
   when actual work completes. A quiet-frame gate improves presentation but cannot
   keep rendering through a blocked main thread.

Keep the six independent still pairs as runtime presentation. A continuous movie or six
short video clips still depends on the engine submitting frames; splitting video files
alone does not cure these main-thread stalls. Offline review movies remain convenient.

Acceptance checks for the proposed scheduler: compare High/Low fresh runs using the
same start and viewport; report startup and first-open timings; verify cache hits and no
new runtime bakes; target no sustained frames over 50ms after the unavoidable scene
attachment, with measured exceptions; test cancellation and stale-result rejection;
compare deterministic layouts; test on Windows as well as Metal. The current High/Low
measurements fail that frame-time target. This change does not claim to fix world loading.

Godot's [thread-safe API guidance](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.6/tutorials/performance/thread_safe_apis.html)
requires keeping active SceneTree access out of workers. Its
[background-loading guidance](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.6/tutorials/io/background_loading.html)
also warns that `load_threaded_get()` can block if called before completion. The proposed
split follows those constraints; changing the rendering thread model alone is not a fix.

Use `LOAD_PROF=1 CAROUSEL_PROFILE=1` with the probe for the actual demo menu defaults;
use `CAROUSEL_START=res://data/starts/coal_baron.json` to reproduce the legacy stress
case (the original trace also omitted menu overrides). Add `CAROUSEL_LOW=1`
for the in-memory Low override. `CAROUSEL_IDLE_PROFILE=1` measures the carousel after a
3s warmup. Each writes `profile.json` beside its captures; long-frame records include
absolute timestamps for correlation with the existing world/overlay trace.
